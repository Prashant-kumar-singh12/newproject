Features

Six features, each solving one specific problem.

Open any card to read how it works in practice. Nothing here needs a paid plan — students use the whole platform free.

Skill-based matching

Tell us the languages, frameworks and tools you actually use. Openings are ranked by how closely they overlap with your stack, not by how early you applied.

Each listing is scored against your profile on three things: required skills you already have, skills you are one step away from, and the time commitment you selected. You can see the score breakdown before applying, so a 62% match tells you exactly which two skills to pick up next.

Project-first profile

A resume line says "knows React". A working project proves it. Your profile leads with what you built, with a live link and a short build note.

Import repositories, add a one-paragraph note on the problem you solved, and pin your three strongest projects. Recruiters see the build note first and the marks sheet last, which is the order most of them ask for anyway.

Application tracker

Every application you send sits on one board: applied, shortlisted, interview, offer, closed. No more digging through email to remember who replied.

The board updates when a company changes your status and reminds you when an application has been silent for ten days. You can add your own notes per company, which turns out to be the part students use most before an interview.

Mentor connect

Book a 20-minute call with a senior student or an alum who has already interned where you are applying.

Mentors list the companies they interned at and the two topics they are happy to help with. Slots are short on purpose: a focused 20 minutes on one project or one interview round beats a vague hour.

Resume checker

Paste a job description, get a plain list of what your resume is missing for that specific role.

The checker compares your profile against the posting and flags missing keywords, weak action verbs and sections that run past one page. It never rewrites your resume for you — it tells you what to fix so the wording stays yours.

Interview practice

Round-wise question sets collected from students who sat the same interviews last season.

Sets are grouped by round — aptitude, DSA, core subject, HR — and tagged by company and year. After each set you mark what you got wrong, and those topics show up again a week later.

What a student gets in the first week

The platform is front-loaded on purpose. Most of the value arrives before you send a single application.

  • A profile that reads like a portfolio, not a marks sheet.
  • Your first ten ranked matches with gaps named.
  • One mentor call booked.
  • A resume check against one real posting.

For hiring partners

Companies post an opening once and see candidates ranked by skill overlap, with each student's build notes attached. Listings stay open for 30 days.
